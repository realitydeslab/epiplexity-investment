### Compounded GLP-1 Pharmacies
The biggest losers are compounding pharmacies that were selling knockoff semaglutide. HIMS' pivot from compounding to branded distribution means the compounding market is shrinking AND HIMS is capturing the branded upside.

### Traditional Pharmacies

## Pair Trade Opportunities

### Long HIMS / Short TDOC
- **Thesis:** HIMS is the DTC telehealth winner; TDOC is the legacy loser
- **HIMS** ($5B mcap, 59% growth, profitable) vs. **TDOC** ($945M mcap, declining)
- TDOC has failed to monetize its platform; HIMS has cracked the consumer healthcare code
- Risk: TDOC is already cheap and could bounce on M&A speculation

### Long HIMS / Short CVS
- **Thesis:** DTC telehealth disrupting retail pharmacy distribution
- CVS' retail model is structurally challenged; HIMS' subscription model has better economics
- Risk: CVS has diversified revenue (insurance, PBM)

### Long HIMS / Long NVO (Pair)
- **Thesis:** The deal benefits both — HIMS gets distribution, NVO gets channel
- Both stocks should re-rate as the partnership revenue materializes
- This is a correlation play rather than a hedge

## Competitive Moat Assessment

| Moat Factor | Strength | Notes |
|-------------|----------|-------|
| **Brand** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ | Strong consumer brand in telehealth — rare |
| **DTC Platform** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ | Subscription model, high retention, data moat |
| **Regulatory** | ⭐⭐⭐ | Novo deal provides pharma legitimacy |
| **Network** | ⭐⭐⭐ | 2M+ subscribers, growing |
| **Cost** | ⭐⭐ | Lower than retail pharmacy, but branded drug margins are thin |
| **Switching** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ | Subscription model creates inertia |

**Overall Moat: MEDIUM-STRONG** — HIMS' moat is brand + platform + subscriber lock-in. The Novo deal adds pharma partnership moat that competitors can't easily replicate.
