## Pair Trade Opportunities

### 1. Long LRCX / Short TEL (Tokyo Electron)
- **Thesis:** LRCX gains share at GAA transition, TEL loses in conductor etch
- **Risk:** TEL has strong Japan domestic advantage; currency effects

### 2. Long LRCX / Short AMAT (Applied Materials)
- **Thesis:** LRCX's etch intensity increase from GAA/HBM is a larger tailwind than AMAT's broad exposure
- **Risk:** AMAT is more diversified; AMAT has display business recovery potential
- **Note:** Both have similar market caps (~$264B LRCX vs ~$269B AMAT)

### 3. Long LRCX + MU Basket
- **Thesis:** MU's HBM capex directly = Lam's revenue. Correlated upside from memory AI cycle
- **Risk:** Double exposure to memory cycle; if HBM demand disappoints, both fall

## Market Position Summary

Lam Research occupies the **most defensible position** in semiconductor equipment:
- **~50% etch market share** = near-duopoly with TEL
- **Switching costs are extreme** — fabs qualify equipment for 2-3 years; switching mid-production is nearly impossible
- **Etch intensity increasing** — each new node generation requires MORE etch steps, structurally growing Lam's TAM
- **Customer lock-in** — LRCX's process recipes are integral to customers' production flows
- **No new entrants** — the barrier to entry in semiconductor equipment is essentially infinite (billions in R&D + decades of process knowledge)

## Competitive Moat Rating: A

Lam has one of the widest moats in technology. The only risk is cyclicality — when fabs stop buying, everyone stops buying. But the structural trend of increasing etch intensity means each cycle's peak is higher than the last.
