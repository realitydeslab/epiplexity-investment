*Research date: March 8, 2026*

### Who Loses if LMT Wins?

- **Boeing Defense** (BA) — loses F-35 share; their fighter programs (F/A-18, F-15EX) are legacy
- **European defense** partially — Dassault Rafale, Eurofighter compete with F-35 for foreign orders, but increasingly losing
- **Chinese defense** (AVIC, CASC) — geopolitical rivalry; every F-35 sold to allies is a jet NOT bought from China

### LMT's Moats

1. **F-35 monopoly** — only manufacturer of the Western world's primary 5th-gen fighter. No competition.
2. **THAAD/PAC-3 missile defense** — limited competition (RTX on some systems)
3. **Space intelligence** — classified NRO/NGA satellites, deep security clearances
4. **$166B backlog** — 2.2x annual revenue locked in
5. **Switching costs** — once a country buys F-35s, they're locked into LMT for decades of sustainment

### Pair Trade Opportunities

**Long LMT / Short BA (Boeing)**
- Rationale: LMT executing on F-35 ramp while Boeing struggles with quality issues, 737 MAX aftermath, Starliner delays
- Boeing's defense segment losing money; LMT's defense is profitable
- Risk: Boeing recovery could squeeze the short

**Long LMT / Long KTOS**
- Rationale: Both benefit from drone warfare + Iran conflict, but different size/risk profiles
- LMT = steady compounder, KTOS = high-beta pure play on autonomous combat
- Barbell approach to defense AI

### Sector Dynamics
- Defense stocks have been the "safe haven" trade in 2025-2026: outperforming S&P by ~30%
- Iran conflict is the primary driver; European rearmament is secondary
- **Risk:** if conflict ends, the entire sector could see 10-15% pullback as war premium unwinds
- **Counterargument:** Post-conflict restocking takes years (see: post-Ukraine defense budgets still rising 3 years later)
