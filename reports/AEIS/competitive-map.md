## Who LOSES if AEIS Wins?

1. **Delta Electronics (2308.TW)** — Direct competitor in data center power supplies. If AEIS gains share with hyperscalers via OCP-compliant designs and Thailand cost advantages, Delta loses.
2. **Lite-On Power Technology** — Taiwan-based server PSU maker; AEIS's expanding product line threatens their position.
3. **Bel Fuse (BELFB)** — Smaller power products company that competes in some segments.
4. **Generic power supply makers** — As data centers demand higher efficiency (titanium-rated PSUs), commodity players lose to precision companies like AEIS.

## Pair Trade Opportunities

### Long AEIS / Short MKSI
- **Thesis:** Both serve semi equipment, but AEIS has the data center growth kicker that MKSI lacks. AEIS is diversifying away from semi cyclicality; MKSI remains more pure-play semi equipment.
- **Risk:** If semi equipment recovers faster than data center grows, MKSI outperforms.

### Long AEIS / Short Generic Power (BELFB)
- **Thesis:** Precision power conversion companies gain share as data center power requirements become more complex (48V architecture, liquid cooling integration). Commodity power makers lose.
- **Risk:** Generic players could innovate or compete on price.

### Complementary Position: AEIS + VRT
- **Not a pair trade** — these are complementary, not opposing. AEIS = power conversion, VRT = power distribution + cooling. Both benefit from same data center capex cycle. Consider owning both for full AI power infrastructure exposure.

2. **Vertiv** could backward-integrate into power conversion
3. **Hyperscalers** could design power systems in-house (like they did with custom silicon)
