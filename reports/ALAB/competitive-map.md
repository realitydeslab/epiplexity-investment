# ALAB Competitive Map — Astera Labs

*Research date: March 8, 2026*

## Direct Competitors

### Credo Technology (CRDO)
- **Overlap:** Active electrical cables, high-speed connectivity for AI
- **Threat level:** High — direct competitor in AEC market
- **Difference:** Credo focuses more on SerDes/PHY IP and line card retimers. Astera has broader portfolio (retimers + cables + switches + CXL).
- **Note:** CRDO fell 9.15% on Jan 22 when ALAB announced Scorpio production — market sees them as competitive.

### Marvell Technology (MRVL)
- **Overlap:** Custom AI silicon, networking, switches
- **Threat level:** Medium-High — larger, more diversified, but less focused on AI connectivity
- **Difference:** Marvell does custom ASICs and broader networking. Astera is pure-play AI connectivity.
- **Scorpio vs Marvell switches:** Marvell has its own switch portfolio, but Astera's "smart fabric" approach with software (COSMOS) differentiates.

### Broadcom (AVGO)
- **Overlap:** Networking, switching, PCIe solutions
- **Threat level:** Medium — Broadcom is the 800-lb gorilla in networking silicon
- **Difference:** Broadcom serves broader markets. Astera is more nimble and AI-focused. Broadcom's Jericho/Ramon switches compete with Scorpio at scale.

### Microchip Technology / Montage Technology
- **Overlap:** PCIe retimers, CXL controllers
- **Threat level:** Low-Medium — smaller players, less AI credibility

## Who Benefits from the SAME Catalysts?

If AI connectivity bottleneck narrative accelerates:
- **CRDO** (Credo) — direct beneficiary
- **MRVL** (Marvell) — networking/custom silicon
- **GLW** (Corning) — fiber optic backbone
- **ANET** (Arista Networks) — AI networking switches
- **NVDA** (NVIDIA) — more GPUs = more connectivity needed

## Who Loses if Astera Wins?

- **Broadcom** (share loss in switching if Scorpio gains traction)
- **Generic cable/connector makers** (replaced by smart active cables)
- **Older retimer players** (can't match Gen6 performance)

## Pair Trade Opportunities

### Long ALAB / Short CRDO
- **Thesis:** Astera has a broader portfolio (retimers + cables + switches + CXL) vs. Credo's narrower AEC/SerDes focus. Scorpio gives Astera a growth vector Credo doesn't have.
- **Risk:** Credo could win share in 800G AEC specifically.

### Long ALAB / Long GLW (Basket)
- **Thesis:** Both benefit from the connectivity bottleneck theme. Astera = chip-level, Corning = fiber-level. Not competitive — complementary.

## Competitive Moat Assessment

1. **Software moat (COSMOS):** Platform creates switching costs. Customers integrate COSMOS into their operating stacks.
2. **First-mover in Gen6:** "Probably the only company" shipping PCIe Gen6 retimers in volume.
3. **Full-stack connectivity:** Only company offering retimers + AECs + switches + CXL controllers. One-stop shop.
4. **Hyperscaler relationships:** Amazon warrant ($466M), design wins with NVIDIA, AMD, Intel, hyperscalers.
5. **Execution speed:** Went from IPO to $852M revenue in under 2 years.

**Moat Rating: Strong but young.** The software moat is real but hasn't been tested through a downcycle. Customer concentration remains a vulnerability.
