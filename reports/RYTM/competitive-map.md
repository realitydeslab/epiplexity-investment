# RYTM Competitive Map 🗺️

*Rhythm Pharmaceuticals — Last Updated: March 9, 2026*

## Direct Competitors in MC4R Pathway
- **None.** Rhythm has the only approved MC4R agonist (setmelanotide/IMCIVREE). This is a genuine first-mover monopoly in rare genetic obesity.

## Who Benefits from the SAME Catalysts?
- **Rare disease sector broadly** — an HO approval validates FDA's willingness to approve drugs in ultra-rare metabolic conditions
- **Genetic testing companies** (Invitae, 23andMe remnants) — more genetic obesity diagnoses = more patients identified
- **MC4R pathway companies** — validates the biological target

## Who Loses if RYTM Wins?
- No direct losers — HO is uncontested. RYTM creates a NEW market rather than taking share.
- Indirectly: GLP-1 companies may see less attention in rare obesity if IMCIVREE establishes itself as standard of care

## Competitive Moat Assessment
- **Regulatory moat:** Orphan Drug Designation provides 7-year market exclusivity for approved rare indications
- **Patent protection:** Setmelanotide composition patents + method-of-use patents
- **Clinical data moat:** Decades of clinical development; competitor would need 5+ years to replicate
- **Patient identification moat:** Rhythm has built the rare obesity diagnostic infrastructure (LEAD for Rare Obesity program)
- **Moat durability:** HIGH for current indications; MEDIUM if oral bivamelagon proves out (broader market = more competition)

## Key Insight
RYTM's competitive position is unusually strong because they're creating markets that didn't exist — not competing in existing ones. The risk isn't competition; it's whether the markets are big enough to justify the $6.4B valuation.
