## Who Loses if RTX Wins?

- **GE Aerospace (GE):** If P&W's GTF recovery accelerates, GE loses relative engine market share
- **Boeing (BA):** Not directly, but RTX's Collins Aerospace division benefits when airlines keep older aircraft (bad for new Boeing orders)
- **Chinese defense firms:** If Western rearmament accelerates, it's partially a response to Chinese military buildup

## Pair Trade Opportunities

### Long RTX / Short GE
- Thesis: P&W GTF recovery narrows GE's engine dominance
- Risk: GE has its own strong tailwinds (LEAP engine demand)
- Correlation: ~0.5

### Long RTX / Long RHM.DE
- Thesis: U.S. + European defense spending super-cycle. Both benefit from different geographies.
- Not a pair trade — a basket approach

### Long RTX / Short BA
- Thesis: RTX benefits from aging fleet aftermarket; Boeing suffers from production delays
- Risk: Boeing recovery could be dramatic if 737 MAX production normalizes

## RTX's Competitive Moats
1. **Patriot missile system** — only game in town for allied air defense
2. **Pratt & Whitney F135** — sole engine for F-35 (most produced Western fighter)
3. **Tomahawk cruise missile** — no credible alternative for precision strike
4. **Collins Aerospace** — avionics in majority of commercial/military aircraft
5. **Scale** — $88.6B revenue, 180K employees. Only Boeing is larger in A&D.

## Sector Dynamics
- Defense primes are oligopolistic — 5 companies (RTX, LMT, NOC, GD, BA) control ~80% of U.S. defense spend
- High barriers to entry (security clearances, decades of IP, customer relationships)
- Iran conflict benefits ALL primes, but missile/munitions makers (RTX, LMT) benefit most directly
