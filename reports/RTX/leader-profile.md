# RTX Corporation (RTX) — Leader Profile 👤

*Research Date: March 8, 2026*

## CEO: Christopher Calio

- **Role:** CEO since April 2024, succeeding Greg Hayes
- **Background:** Long-time RTX insider. Previously served as President and COO. Led Pratt & Whitney division through the challenging GTF engine recall and recovery.
- **Execution Track Record:** FY2025 was his first full year as CEO and delivered outstanding results — revenue $88.6B (+9.7%), net income $6.73B (+41%), FCF $7.94B (+75%). Clearly executing on the operational side.
- **Strategic Focus:** Accelerating delivery on defense backlog, ramping Pratt & Whitney production, deleveraging the balance sheet.
- **Assessment:** RIGHT PERSON for this moment. Deep operational knowledge, inherited a massive backlog, and is converting it into cash flow effectively.

## Key Leadership Moves

- Navigated P&W GTF powder metal recall without catastrophic financial impact
- Drove operating margins from 5.2% (FY2023) to 10.5% (FY2025) — remarkable recovery
- FCF conversion improved dramatically ($4.5B → $7.9B in one year)

## Insider Activity

- Defense sector insiders are typically net sellers due to equity compensation structures
- No material open-market insider buying signals detected in recent filings
- However, the stock has nearly doubled from its April 2025 52-week low of $112.27 to current $209.76, suggesting insiders who held have been vindicated
- RTX returned capital primarily through dividends ($3.57B in FY2025) and modest buybacks ($50M)

## Key Hires/Departures

- Greg Hayes transition to Christopher Calio was smooth (April 2024)
- Stable C-suite through FY2025 — no notable departures
- Collins Aerospace leadership driving CCA/autonomy wins

## Public Statements on Near-Term Outlook

- Management guided for continued double-digit growth in defense segment
- Highlighted multi-year backlog visibility as a key differentiator
- Emphasized commercial aerospace aftermarket recovery as a secondary growth driver
- Note: RTX is the world's 2nd largest defense contractor (after Boeing) by revenue at $88.6B

## Red Flags
- None significant. Clean execution story under new CEO.
- Balance sheet still carries $39.5B in debt (legacy of United Technologies/Raytheon merger), but aggressively deleveraging.
