# BAE Systems (BAESY) — Leader Profile 👤

*Research Date: 2026-03-09*

## CEO: Charles Woodburn

- **Tenure:** CEO since July 2017 (~9 years)
- **Background:** Engineering PhD, formerly COO of BAE, prior roles at Shell, Schlumberger, and Expro International
- **Age:** ~55 (appointed at ~46, now experienced through multiple defense cycles)

### Track Record Under Woodburn

- **Revenue:** From ~$22.5B (2018) to $38B TTM — a 69% increase
- **Net Income:** From $1.34B (2018) to $2.5B (2024) — an 87% increase
- **Stock Price:** From ~$30 (2018) to $122 — a 300%+ return
- **Strategic Moves:**
  - Ball Aerospace acquisition ($5.5B, 2024) — expanded space/missile defense
  - Maintained Saudi relationship through ESG pressure
  - Navigated Brexit supply chain challenges
  - Positioned company for AUKUS and GCAP mega-programs
  - Delivered record £83.6B backlog

### Assessment

Woodburn is a quiet, methodical operator — not a showman. He's built a multi-decade order book across five divisions (Electronic Systems, Platforms & Services, Air, Maritime, Cyber & Intelligence). He inherited a UK-centric defense conglomerate and transformed it into a genuinely global platform.

**Key strength:** Capital allocation discipline. The Ball Aerospace deal was the biggest acquisition in BAE's recent history, executed at a reasonable price, and integration is on track.

**Key risk:** Woodburn's strategy is steady-state — no radical pivots, no startup energy. In a world where defense innovation is accelerating (AI, autonomy, cyber), BAE may need more aggressive R&D positioning.

### Chair: Cressida Hogg (Baroness Hogg)

- Appointed Chair 2020
- Former senior civil servant, private equity background (3i Group)
- Strong government connections — valuable for a company dependent on UK MOD relationships

## CEO Public Statements on Near-Term Outlook

From the Feb 18, 2026 results announcement:
- "Another year of outstanding performance" — £83.6B record backlog
- 12% underlying operating profit growth
- Guided for continued "mid-to-high single digit" revenue growth
- Emphasized "unprecedented demand environment" driven by geopolitical tensions
- Highlighted AUKUS, GCAP, and European defense spending as multi-decade tailwinds

**Assessment:** Woodburn's guidance language ("mid-to-high single digit") appears deliberately conservative given the backlog trajectory. This is typical BAE — underpromise, overdeliver. The gap between conservative guidance and the actual demand environment creates upside potential.
