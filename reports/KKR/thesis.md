## THE CATALYST

KKR is down 42% from its 52-week high, caught in the private credit sector panic triggered by Blue Owl's redemption crisis, BlackRock gating withdrawals, and Jamie Dimon's "cockroach" warning. **The market is treating KKR as a pure-play credit lender when it's actually the most diversified alternative asset manager in the world** — with PE, infrastructure, insurance, real estate, and capital markets generating revenue across multiple uncorrelated streams.

The catalyst for re-rating: **FY2026 EPS is expected to nearly TRIPLE from $2.34 to $6.59**, driven by $19B in embedded gains, record $129B fundraising, and the K Series private wealth platform doubling annually. Every quarterly earnings report in 2026 should chip away at the fear discount.

**What kills the trade:**
1. Private credit crisis becomes systemic — KKR's credit funds face redemption pressure or material write-downs
2. FY2026 EPS disappoints — $19B embedded gains don't monetize as expected
3. Macro recession — Beta of 1.99 means 2x downside in a broad selloff
4. Co-CEOs stop buying — If the insider buying signal reverses, the bullish thesis weakens

**Risk mitigants:**
- Co-CEOs bought 175K+ shares at the lows ($8.8M tranche on March 4 alone)
- Global Atlantic insurance provides $200B+ in permanent, non-redeemable capital
- 69% FRE margin and 24% management fee growth show the fee business is thriving
- PEG of 0.49 = growth significantly underpriced

## THE VERDICT

**BUY — High conviction, contrarian entry in a best-in-class alt manager.**

KKR at $88.65 with a forward P/E of 11.25x is pricing in a private credit catastrophe that's unlikely to hit KKR specifically. The co-CEOs are telling you this with their own money. The embedded gains, record fundraising, and K Series growth make the EPS inflection nearly inevitable. The only question is timing — and with Q1 2026 earnings ~2 months away, you don't have to wait long for confirmation.

**Position size: 4-6% of portfolio.** Higher conviction = larger size, but respect the 1.99 beta and sector contagion risk.
